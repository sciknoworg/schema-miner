process name: Catalytic Pyrolysis of Mixed Plastic Waste
process description: Catalytic pyrolysis is a thermal recycling process that breaks down plastic waste into valuable fuels and chemicals using heat and catalysts. Unlike traditional incineration that just burns plastics, pyrolysis heats plastic waste (typically 300-600°C) in the absence of oxygen, causing the long polymer chains to crack into smaller molecules like oils, gases, and waxes. Catalysts (special materials that speed up reactions) help control which products form and improve efficiency. The process takes mixed plastic waste—like bottles, packaging, and films that are difficult to recycle mechanically—and converts them into liquid fuels similar to gasoline or diesel, gases for energy, and even chemical building blocks to make new plastics. Key parameters include temperature, heating rate, catalyst type, residence time, and plastic feedstock composition. The outputs are typically liquid oil (40-80% yield), gas, and solid char, with properties varying based on operating conditions.
